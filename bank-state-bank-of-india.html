AI underwrites MSME loans through the Business Rule Engine

Announced August 2026

SBI used AI to underwrite nearly ₹1 trillion (₹1 lakh crore) of loans to small and mid-sized businesses in the 2025-26 financial year. That makes it one of the largest AI lending operations anywhere.

What it is

SBI's managing director Rama Mohan Rao Amara spoke at the FIBAC 2026 conference. He said the bank underwrote almost ₹1 trillion of MSME loans, each up to ₹5 crore, in the twelve months of FY26. The loans went to both new and existing customers.

How it works

The bank's Business Rule Engine combines GST network and GST filing data, account information, credit bureau scores and other structured and unstructured data to assess each borrower. The same AI approach is also used for unsecured lending to thin-file customers such as small businesses and proprietors.

Technology used

SBI's Business Rule Engine (BRE) with AI models; the source names no external model vendor.

Benefit reported (Bank-stated aim)

Amara said the portfolio underwritten through the Business Rule Engine had lower non-performing assets (NPAs) than the bank's wider book; no figure was given.

Plans AI scrutiny of LC documents and agentic drafting of smart LCs and guarantees

Announced May 2026

SBI's annual report lists AI use cases for trade finance, including document scrutiny for LC and bill documents. Agentic AI (software that carries out tasks on its own) would generate smart LCs and bank guarantees.

What it is

In its IT section, the annual report says AI will be leveraged to streamline trade finance processes and products. The named use cases sit inside the bank's Trade Finance NextLeap programme, run from two centralised Global Trade Finance Centres.

How it works

The listed use cases are inland bank guarantee classification and text vetting, document classification across all trade finance modules, and document scrutiny for LCs and bills. A further use case applies agentic AI to generate smart LCs and guarantees.

Vision-LLM processes cheques straight through

Announced August 2026

SBI built an AI model that reads cheques, checks their compliance fields and processes small cheques automatically, with almost no human touch. Cheques up to ₹10,000 are about a quarter of all SBI cheques.

What it is

The model was described by managing director Rama Mohan Rao Amara at FIBAC 2026 in August 2026. Cheques of ₹10,000 or less account for around 25 per cent of the bank's cheque volumes.

How it works

A vision large language model reads the cheque image and verifies the mandatory fields and compliance requirements. Passing cheques go through a straight-through processing (STP) system with practically no human involvement. A dedicated control risk unit reviews a sample of AI-processed cheques to spot errors and decide whether the model needs more training.

Technology used

Vision LLMs (large language models that take images as input), built by SBI; no external vendor named in the source.

Benefit reported (Not stated)

The source does not state a benefit. The bank said the automated process works at "a quite very good threshold" of efficiency, with no figure given.

Source (Independent reporting): SBI used AI to underwrite nearly ₹1 trillion MSME loans in FY26: Amara" (Business Standard, 12 August 2026)

AI bot handling customer care calls end to end

Announced August 2026

At SBI's customer care centre, an AI bot now handles customer calls end to end. Only complex calls are passed to a human agent.

What it is

Managing director Rama Mohan Rao Amara said at FIBAC 2026 that several of SBI's generative AI experiments had moved into full-scale use. Customer service is one of them: the AI bot takes the call itself rather than only helping a human agent.

How it works

The bot handles the customer's call from start to finish. Calls it judges complex are transferred to human agents.

Technology used

Generative AI; the source names no model or vendor.

Benefit reported (Not stated)

The source does not state a benefit.
