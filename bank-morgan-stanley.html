Wealth super agents take on client tasks under advisor approval

Announced February 2026

Morgan Stanley is building wealth management 'super agents' from hundreds of smaller agents across more than 3,500 tools. They will answer client questions, open accounts and move money. Advisors approve each recommendation first.

What it is

Wealth head Jed Finn described the super agents at a February 2026 conference, alongside a tool called the Roth Conversion Analyst. The Roth tool pulls in client data and the advisor's assumptions, then recommends whether a client should convert a retirement account. Finn likened the top tier of agents to a 'Jarvis' for managing money.

How it works

Smaller agents each handle a defined task, such as answering balance questions or sending tax documents. Combined agents can assemble portfolios from the firm's investment options against criteria the advisor sets. Nothing is implemented until the advisor reviews and approves it.

Opening ShareWorks and Equity Edge stock-plan platforms to clients' AI agents

Announced June 2026

Morgan Stanley will let corporate clients' own AI agents connect directly to its ShareWorks and Equity Edge stock-plan platforms. External AI agents get system access, among the first moves by a Wall Street bank.

What it is

ShareWorks and Equity Edge are the platforms companies use to run their employee stock plans with Morgan Stanley at Work. Morgan Stanley plans to open both platforms to autonomous AI agents used by its corporate clients.

How it works

Clients' AI agents will pull data and insights directly from the platforms, bypassing the traditional human login screens. The connection uses the Model Context Protocol, an open-source standard that lets AI models connect to data sources; a small group of clients already has early access.

Technology used

Model Context Protocol (MCP); clients' own agentic AI tools connecting to ShareWorks and Equity Edge.

Benefit reported (Not stated)

The source does not state a benefit. It states a rollout plan, to all 3,400 administration clients by the following year, but no measured benefit.

FIXR agents halve end-of-day P&L reconciliation

Announced June 2026

Morgan Stanley's FIXR system reconciles trading profit and loss every night. Work that took up to six hours per book now takes two to three hours. That saves about 1,500 controller hours a week.

What it is

FIXR is Morgan Stanley's internal production system for end-of-day profit and loss reconciliation. Controllers must match hundreds of thousands of attributes across Finance, Risk, Operations and Trade Capture systems. Mismatches, called breaks, previously needed manual investigation against a hard morning deadline.

How it works

After nightly calculations finish, FIXR analyses breaks and proposes resolutions based on learned rules. Three agents cooperate: one drafts start-of-day resolutions from past guidance, one documents rules controllers apply, one converts repeated patterns into automated logic. Controllers review, approve or correct every recommendation.

Technology used

FIXR, Morgan Stanley's in-house multi-agent system using large language models. The source does not name the specific models.

Benefit reported (Measured result)

Reconciliation per book fell from up to six hours to two or three hours. Across roughly 100 controllers, that saves about 1,500 hours a week.

Service assist agent for wealth management client service associates

Announced October 2026

Morgan Stanley's wealth management division runs a "service assist" agent that works as a companion to its client service associates. It can also take actions, such as moving money, opening accounts and changing beneficiaries.

What it is

Hina Shamsi, CIO of wealth management technology, described three focus areas for the division's agents — client interaction, service and adviser assist. The service assist agent supports the client service associates who handle client servicing work, and was live by October 2026.

How it works

The agent retrieves information and takes actions across the firm's systems, doing work that previously required moving across multiple applications. It operates within clearly defined boundaries, with permissions, monitoring and human oversight.

Technology used

Morgan Stanley's wealth management AI agents. The source does not name the underlying model for this agent.

Benefit reported (Bank-stated aim)

Shamsi said the division is seeing productivity gains with the service assist agent. No figures were given.

DevGen.AI

Announced January 2025

DevGen.AI translates old computer code into plain English so developers can rewrite it in modern languages. It tackles one of the bank's biggest technology problems: modernising decades-old systems firm-wide.

What it is

DevGen.AI is a generative AI tool Morgan Stanley built itself and introduced in January 2025. It translates code in older languages, such as Perl, into plain English specifications.

How it works

Developers use the plain-English output as the basis for rewriting the code in newer languages such as Python. The tool was built on OpenAI's GPT models and trained on the languages in Morgan Stanley's own code base, including company-specific ones. Developers stay in the loop because the tool does not yet write new code as well as a human.

Technology used

OpenAI GPT models (in-house tool, trained on Morgan Stanley's own code base).

Benefit reported (Measured result)

In the five months after launch, DevGen.AI had worked through nine million lines of code, saving the firm's 15,000 developers roughly 280,000 hours of work (Mike Pizzi, global head of technology and operations, told the Wall Street Journal, as reported in the source below).
