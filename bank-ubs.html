AI analyst avatars ("analyst clones")

Announced May 2025 (reported 20 May 2025; rollout started January 2025) · Went live January 2025

UBS creates lifelike AI video avatars of its investment-bank research analysts. The avatars turn written research into videos, because clients increasingly want research in video form.

What it is

Analysts can have a digital version of themselves, in likeness and voice, present their research on video without going into a studio each time. The programme is opt-in, was approved by UBS HR, and every avatar video needs the analyst's own approval before it goes to clients and is labelled as AI-created.

How it works

The analyst records their likeness and voice in a studio, where Synthesia captures them. A language model then analyses the analyst's written report and drafts a video script, the analyst reviews the script, and it is turned into a lifelike avatar video.

Technology used

OpenAI language models and Synthesia avatar technology.

Benefit reported (Measured result)

UBS said it was targeting about 5,000 avatar videos a year once fully running, versus about 1,000 videos a year from its existing studio capacity (it publishes about 50,000 written documents a year). This is a stated target, not a reported achieved result.

Source (Independent reporting): "UBS deploys AI analyst clones" — Financial Times, republished by SWI swissinfo.ch, 20 May 2025

Analytics tools give call centre staff instant insights

Announced March 2026

UBS uses analytics tools in its call centres that give staff instant insights while they serve clients. The group's own annual report names it as a working AI use case, though it discloses no numbers.

What it is

The Letter to Shareholders in the UBS Annual Report 2025 (published March 2026) states: "our analytics tools in call centers are providing instant insights to improve service and boost efficiency".

How it works

The tools surface insights to call centre staff during their work, so they can serve clients better and faster. The report gives no further operational detail.

RiskLab expansion — Domino Data Lab AI model platform rolled out bank-wide

Announced August 2025 (21 August 2025)

UBS expanded RiskLab, its platform for building and checking AI models, and is rolling it out bank-wide. UBS also invested in the platform's maker, Domino Data Lab, and took a board observer seat.

What it is

RiskLab is UBS's platform for AI model development and validation, built inside UBS over more than five years on Domino's product. The August 2025 expansion takes that approach bank-wide so teams across UBS can build and deploy AI models on a shared, governed foundation.

How it works

The platform gives UBS teams a secure, collaborative and auditable environment for developing models, with full traceability and model governance. This supports UBS's risk-based approach to model development as AI use scales across the firm.

Technology used

Domino Data Lab Enterprise AI Platform (basis for UBS RiskLab).

Benefit reported (Not stated)

The source does not state a benefit. (The announcement says the rollout is expected to streamline model development and deployment, but gives no figures.)

Source (Independent reporting): "UBS Invests in Domino Data Lab, Deepening Strategic Partnership to Drive Next Wave of Enterprise AI Innovation" — joint Domino Data Lab / UBS announcement, 21 August 2025

UBS Red — in-house generative AI assistant

Announced December 2024 (rolled out to 30,000 employees as of December 2024; UBS's 16 October 2025 news release later confirmed Red and M365 Copilot were being rolled out to all employees, with over 300 live AI use cases)

UBS built its own AI assistant, called Red, for staff across the bank. It lets employees find and use the bank's own internal knowledge much faster in their daily work.

What it is

Red is a generative AI assistant developed by UBS itself, not an off-the-shelf product bought in. It was rolled out across Personal & Corporate Banking, Wealth Management Switzerland, Global Wealth Management, and the Group Operations and Technology Office. The booking centres covered were Switzerland, Hong Kong and Singapore.

How it works

Staff use Red inside the bank's systems, running on Microsoft Azure. Within 10 months the underlying Azure OpenAI Service had reached 30,000 employees, supported by reserved computing capacity (provisioned throughput units) to keep response times low and predictable.

Technology used

Microsoft Azure OpenAI Service (Microsoft Azure cloud platform).

Benefit reported (Not stated)

The source does not state a benefit. (The Microsoft story describes wider data-platform modernisation following Red's success, but gives no time-saving or cost figure.)

Source (Independent reporting): "UBS and Microsoft unite: Co-creating the future of banking with Azure AI" — Microsoft Customer Stories
