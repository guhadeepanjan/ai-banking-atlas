LiveCorporate Banking

AIDox automatically processes commercial client emails

Announced April 2026

Scotiabank's AIDox system processes commercial banking emails automatically. It reads each message and directs it to the right team, so client instructions no longer wait for staff to sort them by hand.

What it is

AIDox was highlighted by the bank at the launch of Scotia Intelligence in April 2026 as one of its working agentic AI systems in commercial banking.

How it works

The system processes about 90 per cent of commercial emails and routes them to the appropriate teams, so staff handle the request itself rather than the sorting.

Technology used

Agentic AI within the bank's Scotia Intelligence programme; the underlying models are not named in the source.

Benefit reported (Measured result)

The bank said AIDox reduced manual work by 70 per cent and shortened turnaround times, as reported in coverage of the Scotia Intelligence launch.

Source (Independent reporting): Scotiabank launches Scotia Intelligence for bank-wide AI" (IT Brief, reporting Scotiabank's launch announcement, April 2026)

LivePayments & Remittances

Five AI agents for payments operations and the Client Insight Report

Announced September 2025

Scotiabank's Global Transaction Banking team built five AI agents, with EY and Microsoft, that turn messy client payment data into a Client Insight Report. Work that once took weeks now takes seconds.

What it is

The bank published the case study through its Global Transaction Banking business on 17 September 2025. The five agents cover data transformation, reconciliation, exception handling, aggregation and report preparation.

How it works

Working like a digital assembly line, each agent cleans and structures payment data, matches and reconciles it, and flags exceptions. The agents then assemble the report, analysing what was processed, what failed and what remediation is needed. The prototype was built in under three months on Microsoft's Copilot platform.

Technology used

Microsoft Copilot platform, built with EY and Microsoft.

Benefit reported (Measured result)

The bank said the report work that took weeks now takes seconds; at Sibos 2025 its managing director of global payments said reconciliation reports that once took up to 20 hours are now largely automated.

Source (Independent reporting): Canada's Frontier Firms Are Emerging and They're Redefining AI Leadership" (Microsoft Source Canada, reporting Scotiabank's Global Transaction Banking case study of 17 September 2025)

Scotia Intelligence: expanded enterprise AI suite for over 71,000 employees

Announced June 2026 · Went live April 2026

Scotiabank expanded Scotia Intelligence, its unified data and AI programme, with new tools including Notebooks, Create and Pages. Over 71,000 employees now have access to its assistive AI tools.

What it is

Scotia Intelligence is the bank's unified approach to data and AI, built around client experience, speed and efficiency, and risk management. The expansion was announced on 10 June 2026, following the programme's launch in April 2026.

How it works

Employees use the tools to find curated policy and knowledge content, synthesise complex information into digestible content with human review, and write code. Notebooks brings files, emails, meeting notes and data into one AI workspace that produces summaries and drafts; Create produces presentations; Pages turns AI responses into editable shared documents.

Technology used

Scotiabank's Scotia Intelligence platform and Scotia Navigator assistant suite; underlying model providers are not named in the release.

Benefit reported (Measured result)

The bank reported a 30 per cent quarter-over-quarter increase in the use of AI by employees to reply to client questions; no financial benefit figure was given.

Source (Bank announcement): Scotiabank Accelerates Enterprise Adoption of AI with New Scotia Intelligence Capabilities" (Scotiabank press release via Canada NewsWire, 10 June 2026)
