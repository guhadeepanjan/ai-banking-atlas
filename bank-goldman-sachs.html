Pilots Marquee AI, a grounded research assistant, inside the firm

Announced August 2026

Goldman is piloting Marquee AI, a natural-language assistant on its Marquee platform for institutional clients. It is available only inside the firm for now, ahead of a planned client launch.

What it is

Marquee AI sits on Marquee, the firm's digital platform for institutional and corporate clients. Staff ask targeted questions in natural language, and the system assembles a grounded answer from the firm's own material.

How it works

It breaks each question into relevant research topics, then finds matching research, trading floor commentary and Market View analytics. Where a calculation is needed, it pulls a data widget and runs code to produce the answer. Every sentence in the output is attributed to a specific line in a source, so users can audit it.

Technology used

Frontier large language models, plus a Goldman-built grounding framework that ties every generated sentence to a specific source line.

Launches AlphaAI investing platform across public and private markets

Announced July 2026

Goldman Sachs Asset Management has launched AlphaAI, a platform that applies AI across its public and private markets investing. Lou D'Ambrosio leads it as chairman of AI for Asset Management.

What it is

AlphaAI is an investing platform launched by Goldman Sachs Asset Management in July 2026. The firm is betting that AI becomes an important driver of investment returns across its public and private market businesses.

How it works

The platform draws on what Goldman sees across the breadth of its public and private markets business. It also uses lessons from more than 100 scaled AI use cases inside its portfolio companies. D'Ambrosio argues AI will drive greater dispersion within sectors, and AlphaAI is built to find it.

Claude-based autonomous agents for trade/transaction accounting and client vetting & onboarding

Announced February 2026

Goldman Sachs spent six months co-developing autonomous agents with Anthropic engineers embedded in its teams. They take agentic AI (software that carries out tasks on its own) beyond coding into accounting, compliance and client vetting.

What it is

Autonomous agents for two areas: accounting for trades and transactions, and client vetting and onboarding. Status caveat: in February 2026 Argenti said the firm was still in the "early stages" of developing the agents and expected to launch them soon. It was an announced co-development/testing programme, not a confirmed full production launch in that source.

How it works

Anthropic engineers were embedded within Goldman's technology teams to co-develop agents that can perform complex, rule-based tasks. The agents were being tested on transaction reconciliation, trade accounting, and client vetting and onboarding, which require processing large volumes of data against strict regulatory frameworks.

Technology used

Anthropic Claude

The source does not state a benefit. (Argenti said the agents would collapse the amount of time these functions take, but no measured figure was given in the February 2026 report.)

AnnouncedEnterprise-wide

Devin — autonomous AI software engineer (Cognition)

Announced July 2025

Goldman Sachs began piloting Devin, Cognition's autonomous AI coding agent, alongside its human developers. Goldman was reported as the first major bank to use a start-to-finish autonomous coding agent in this way.

What it is

An AI software engineer that does tasks on behalf of developers, as part of what CIO Marco Argenti called a "hybrid workforce." A named example task is updating internal code to newer programming languages.

How it works

Devin instances work under human supervision on software-engineering tasks. Argenti said the bank would start with hundreds of Devin instances, potentially growing to thousands depending on the use cases, alongside around 12,000 human developers.

Technology used

Devin, by Cognition

Benefit reported (Not stated)

The source does not state a benefit. (Argenti said he hoped it would improve developer productivity, but the July 2025 report gives no measured gain.)

GS AI Assistant — firmwide launch

Announced June 2025

Goldman Sachs launched its generative AI tool, the GS AI Assistant, to all employees, after about 10,000 employees were already using it. It was the firm's first generative AI tool to reach firmwide scale.

What it is

An internal generative AI assistant for employees across the firm. CIO Marco Argenti's memo called it "an important moment in our AI journey" and "the first generative AI-powered tool to reach this scale."

How it works

Employees use it to summarise complex documents, draft initial content, perform data analysis and translate documents. It was tailored for functions including investment banking, software development, research analysis, and asset and wealth management.

Technology used

Generative AI on Goldman's GS AI Platform (specific model not named in the source)

Benefit reported (Not stated)

The source does not state a benefit. (The source states the aim was to improve productivity, but gives no measured figure.)
